# Reply templates

Fill every bracket with this brand's real details. A draft with an unfilled placeholder in it is a bug, not
a draft — the creator shouldn't have to find and fix a `[ ]` before sending.

## Quote / accept / counter reply

```
Hi [name],

Thanks for reaching out about [brand] — [one specific line that proves you read their email, e.g. "congrats
on the launch" or "I've actually used [product] before"].

[If quoting or countering:] For [package name], my rate is $[price]. That includes [1–2 concrete things from
the package description — platforms, length, what's covered].
[If accepting their number:] $[their number] works for [package name] — happy to move ahead.

A few terms, same for every partner:
- [payment terms from the rate card, in plain language]
- [usage rights from the rate card]
- [disclosure line, e.g. "I'll tag it clearly as sponsored, same as everything else I run"]

If that works for you, let me know and I'll send over [a deposit link / next steps]. Happy to answer anything
first.

[sign-off]
```

## Decline (auto-decline match, or just not a fit)

```
Hi [name],

Thanks for thinking of [creator's name/handle] for [brand] — I appreciate you reaching out.

This isn't the right fit for [my audience / what I cover] right now, so I'll pass this time. [Optional, only
if genuinely true: "Feel free to check back if that changes."]

Best,
[sign-off]
```

Never explain an auto-decline in detail (no need to tell a crypto brand why crypto is a no) — the short
version above covers every decline. If the real reason is personal and the creator wants to say more, that's
their call to add, not something to invent on their behalf.

## What never goes in a draft
- A price, date or term that isn't on the rate card or in the brand's own email
- An apology for the creator's rate ("sorry it's a bit much, but…") — state the price plainly
- Exclusivity, perpetual usage rights, or a posting date inside 5 days — those are always a flag, never a
  quiet "sure" buried in a draft
