# Triage rules: deciding what to do with a brand email

Four possible outcomes. Decide which one applies **before** drafting anything — say it out loud in the
report, don't just start writing a reply and see where it ends up.

## 1. Quote list price
Default for a first email with no number from the brand yet, or a number that's already at/above list price.
Quote the package closest to what they asked for at its **list price**, not the floor — never open with a
discount nobody asked for.

## 2. Accept their offer
They named a number **at or above the floor** for the matching package. Accept it plainly, move straight to
payment terms and next steps. Don't keep negotiating once they've already met the floor — that just wastes
everyone's time and can read as greedy.

## 3. Counter once
They named a number **below the floor**. Offer exactly one alternative:
- A smaller package at its list price (e.g. they wanted a dedicated video for short money → offer a short
  instead, at the short's list price), or
- The same package at the floor, if the gap is small and the brand seems serious.

Only one counter. If they counter again below the floor after that, this becomes outcome 4.

## 4. Flag the creator, don't draft a reply
Stop and tell the creator plainly instead of writing anything:
- The brand countered a second time below the floor after outcome 3's counter
- The offer is above the creator's "always flag me" threshold from `creator/rate-card.md` (good news, but
  worth the creator's own voice on a deal that size)
- There's a contract, legal document or signature request attached
- The brand is asking for personal details, banking info or a phone call before any terms are agreed
- The product or brand sits in an ethics grey area the rate card doesn't clearly cover
- Anything genuinely unclear that a template reply would paper over

When flagging, say exactly what's unusual and what the creator needs to decide — don't hedge, don't draft a
"placeholder" reply that pretends a decision got made when it didn't.

## Matching an unlisted ask to a package
Brands rarely ask for exactly what's on the rate card. Match by looking at what the creator actually has to
*do* for it (how much production time, how many platforms, how long it stays live), not the brand's own name
for it. "A mention" from the brand's email might really be a Short, a newsletter slot, or something that
needs a new package entirely — if nothing on the rate card is a reasonable match, say so and flag the
creator rather than forcing a fit.
